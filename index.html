The experiment

Same topic. Two very different requests.

Keep both Copilot conversations available for comparison.

Prompt AAsk for an answer
Why does Earth have seasons?
Notice:
  • Is it easy to understand?
  • Does it help you remember?
  • Does it make you think?
  • What might need verifying?
Prompt BAsk for a tutor
I am a 15-year-old student. Help me learn why Earth has seasons. Start by asking me what I already know. Then explain it in simple language using an everyday analogy. Do not give me more than 120 words at a time. Finish by asking me one question and wait for my answer.
Then:
  • Answer Copilot’s question.
  • Continue for at least one reply.
  • Notice who is doing the thinking.

Compare in pairs

Which version produced more learning?

  1. Which response made you think more?
  2. What did prompt B do differently?
  3. Would you trust either answer without checking?
✓

Verification checkpointThe seasons are mainly caused by Earth’s axial tilt — not by Earth moving closer to or farther from the Sun.

The anatomy of the prompt

Five instructions create the experience.

A long prompt is not automatically a good prompt. Each instruction needs a purpose.

01

Goal

Define exactly what the learner needs to understand or practise.

02

Context

Give the subject, topic, age and current level.

03

Format

Ask for four short scenes and one question at a time.

04

Rules

Control hints, health points, answers and length.

05

Interaction

Make Copilot wait and react to the learner’s choices.

Audio overview

Evidence detective mission

Complete the four lab stations.

You are evaluating the study kit, not testing your ability to create an account.

01

Extract

Write down one claim that appears in at least two resources. Record which two formats included it.

02

Qualify

Find one phrase showing uncertainty or limited evidence. Explain why cautious wording matters more than a confident tone.

03

Test

Choose one claim from the infographic. Check whether the evidence deck and audio repeat, qualify or contradict it. Decide whether the claim looks well supported, uncertain or exaggerated.

04

Challenge

Ask Copilot for a short answer without giving it the teacher’s source set. Compare its answer with the source-based materials.

Without searching the web, answer in 120 words: Has TikTok destroyed teenagers’ attention spans? Separate what is well supported, uncertain and possibly exaggerated. Do not invent sources.

Tool showdown

Which output helped, and which one only looked impressive?

  1. Which format made the topic easiest to understand?
  2. Which format would help you remember it tomorrow?
  3. What important detail disappeared when the information became visual or audio?
  4. Was Copilot more certain than the source-based materials?

The ruleA polished output is not evidence that it is accurate. Trace important claims back to the original source.

04 From notes to retrieval

Build your
AI Study Games.

Turn a short piece of school content into two different retrieval activities, test them with another team and publish the links in your portfolio.

0%lesson complete
The goal is not to generate more content. The goal is to practise remembering.

Memory challenge · books closed

Can you recall five ideas from Session 3 without looking?

Write what you remember first. Then check your notes. The gap between the two is what retrieval practice reveals.

Teacher demonstration · first 7 minutes

One verified text. Two very different games.

  1. Generate a quick recall activity in Wordwall.
  2. Turn the same source into a deeper challenge in Educaplay.
  3. Publish, copy the learner link and test it in a private window.

Today’s rule: AI drafts the activity. You check every answer, improve the questions and decide whether the game genuinely helps someone learn.

Step 1 · Choose the content

Start with a short piece of school material.

Use class notes, a textbook section or a short text provided by your teacher. You do not need to open Gemini Notebook or create any new resources there. Work in pairs if an account or sign-in fails.

1
Choose

Select one clear subtopic — not an entire subject.

2
Collect

Choose 8–12 essential facts from the material. Do not paste a whole chapter.

3
Protect

Remove names, email addresses and personal information before using any external tool.

4
Record

Write down the title or section you used. Add a link only if the material is online.

Steps 2–3 · Creation lab

Build two activities with different jobs.

Do not create the same quiz twice. One should test rapid recall; the other should require deeper thinking.

Tool AQuick recall

Wordwall Sprint

Create one short activity using 8–10 key facts. Choose a format such as Match up, Flashcards, Quiz or Group sort.

  1. Create an account with your school email if permitted, or work with a partner.
  2. Choose Create Activity and an appropriate template.
  3. Use Generate with AI if it appears on your account.
  4. Check every question, answer and distractor before publishing.
Open Wordwall ↗
No AI button?

Use Copilot to draft the content, then paste it into Wordwall manually.

Using only the verified notes below, create 8 concise question-and-answer pairs for retrieval practice. Keep each answer under 12 words. Include no information that is not in my notes. After the questions, flag anything that may be ambiguous. [PASTE YOUR VERIFIED NOTES AND SOURCE TITLE]
Tool BDeeper thinking

Educaplay Challenge

Use Ray, the AI assistant, to create a different kind of challenge from the same verified information.

  1. Choose a format that suits the topic: Fill in the Blanks, Line Up, Matching, Map Quiz, Dialogue or Video Quiz.
  2. Give Ray your verified text rather than only a broad topic.
  3. Review and edit the generated activity.
  4. Publish it and use Share to copy the public game link.
Open Educaplay ↗
Instruction for Ray
Create a short retrieval activity using only the information in this text. Focus on the most important ideas, include plausible distractors and give clear feedback. Do not add facts that are not in the source.

Step 4 · Human quality control

Do not publish the first AI draft.

Inspect both activities and make at least one meaningful improvement.

✓Accuracy

Every correct answer is supported by the source.

?Clarity

There is only one defensible answer unless multiple answers are intended.

↗Challenge

The wrong answers are plausible, not ridiculous.

◎Learning

The activity tests important knowledge, not trivia.

Step 5 · Publish and prove it works

A saved activity is not yet a shareable activity.

01

Publish

Finish and publish each activity. Use a title without your full name.

02

Copy the play link

Choose Share and copy the link intended for players — not an editing link.

03

Test privately

Open a private or InPrivate window, paste the link and check that the activity loads.

04

Add to portfolio

Paste both working links, your source and the improvement you made.

Privacy check: Free activities may be public. Do not include surnames, photographs, school email addresses or personal information.

Peer test

Swap links. Play without help.

  1. Could you open both links without signing in?
  2. Was every question understandable?
  3. Did you find a possible error or ambiguous answer?
  4. Which activity made you retrieve more from memory?
✓

Required repairUse your partner’s feedback to improve at least one question, answer, hint or distractor before completing the portfolio entry.
